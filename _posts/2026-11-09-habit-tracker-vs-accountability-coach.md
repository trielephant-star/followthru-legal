If you search for an app to help with your side project, you’ll find three kinds of tool: habit trackers, to-do and project apps, and accountability apps or coaches. They look similar on an app store page. They solve very different problems.

Here’s an honest look at what each is good at, where each falls short for side projects specifically, and how to pick.

- Habit trackers help you show up, but can’t tell you what to do and punish missed days
- To-do and project apps hold everything, but the list keeps growing
- An accountability coach helps you choose one step, do it, and come back after misses
- Many people combine a project tool for storage with a coach for daily follow-through
{: .summary}

## What a side project actually needs

A side project has a few specific problems that a normal workday doesn’t:

1. **Limited, tired time.** Often 30 to 60 minutes at the end of a long day.
2. **No outside structure.** No manager, deadline or colleague who notices when it stalls.
3. **Constant choice.** There are always 40 things you could do, and picking one takes energy.
4. **Interrupted weeks.** Work, family and life regularly take whole days away.

A good tool for side projects should help with all four. Let’s see how each type does.

## Habit trackers

Habit trackers are built to help you repeat an action: drink water, meditate, read 20 pages. You tick a box each day and build a streak.

**What they’re good at:** making a routine visible. If your problem is simply showing up, a check box can help.

**Where they fall short for side projects:**

- **They track the habit, not the work.** “Worked on my app” gets the same tick whether you shipped a feature or stared at the screen. A side project needs the *right* next step, not just a session.
- **They can’t help you choose.** The tracker doesn’t know your project, so every session still starts with deciding what to do.
- **Streaks punish real life.** With a job and a family, missed days are normal. When a 40-day streak resets to zero, many people feel like they’ve failed and stop entirely.

## To-do and project apps

Notion, Trello, Todoist, a spreadsheet: these hold everything about your project in one place: ideas, tasks, notes, links.

**What they’re good at:** capturing and organizing. They’re excellent as the project’s memory and filing cabinet.

**Where they fall short for side projects:**

- **The list only grows.** Ideas are added faster than tasks are finished, and a 60-item list is discouraging on a tired evening.
- **Everything looks equally urgent.** The tool doesn’t tell you which one task matters most tonight.
- **Nobody checks in.** A project board doesn’t notice when you haven’t opened it in three weeks.

## Accountability apps and coaches

This category is built around follow-through: choosing a step, doing it, and checking in on how it went. A human coach does this in person; an accountability app does it on your phone, every day.

**What they’re good at:**

- **Narrowing to one step.** Instead of a list, you focus on one next thing.
- **Checking in.** Someone (or something) asks how it went, which brings back the outside structure side projects lack.
- **Remembering context.** A good coach remembers what you did last week and what got in your way.
- **Handling misses kindly.** The best ones help you restart after a bad week instead of making you feel behind.

**Where they can fall short:** some accountability apps are really just reminders with a new name, and some lean on public shame or strict streaks, which backfire for many people. Check how the app treats a missed day before you commit.

## How they compare

| | Habit tracker | To-do / project app | Accountability coach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Helps you pick the next step | No | Partly | Yes |
| Knows your project | No | Holds it, doesn’t use it | Yes |
| Checks in on how it went | No | No | Yes |
| Handles missed days | Often resets a streak | Ignores them | Helps you restart |
| Best for | Simple daily routines | Storing everything | Daily follow-through |

## Which should you use?

It depends on where you’re actually stuck:

- **You have a clear plan but don’t sit down:** a simple routine plus some accountability.
- **You sit down but don’t know what to work on:** a tool that narrows your list to one step a day.
- **You start strong and fade after a few weeks:** check-ins and a kind way back after misses.
- **You have notes and ideas everywhere:** a project app to hold it all, plus one of the above for doing.

Many builders end up with two tools: a project app as the filing cabinet, and an accountability app or partner for the daily step.
