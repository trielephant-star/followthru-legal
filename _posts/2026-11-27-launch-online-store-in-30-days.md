You can launch an online store in 30 days at about 30 minutes a day by splitting it into four weeks: choose a few products, build the listings, set up payments and shipping, then open and tell people. "Launched" means open for orders and shared with real people, not perfect.

If you have a job, a family or both, the hard part isn't knowing what a store needs. It's finding the energy after dinner. So every day below is one small Move, with a lighter option for tired nights and a rest day each week.

It works for a mug shop on Etsy, a small Shopify store, or anything in between. The platform matters less than finishing the setup.

- Week 1: pick your products and your buyer, and set your finish line
- Week 2: make three to five product listings, one piece at a time
- Week 3: set up the shop itself: payments, shipping, a test order
- Week 4: open the store, share it, and write down what you learn
- Keep days to 15 to 30 minutes and take one rest day a week
{: .summary}

## What does "launched" mean for a store?

Choose the finish line before day one. Our guide to [defining done](/blog/define-done-for-your-side-project/) explains why this one choice saves weeks.

**Done means: [a specific person] can buy [a specific product] from your store on day 30, and you've asked ten people to look.**

A concrete version: "Three mugs listed with photos, shop open, link sent to ten people." No logo redesign, no email list, no ad budget. Those can wait.

## Week 1: pick a few products and one buyer

You're not choosing a business forever. You're choosing the first three to five things to sell.

1. **Day 1:** Write down what you can make or source. Include what you could ship next week, not only your dream range.
2. **Day 2:** Pick one kind of buyer, such as "people buying a gift for a coffee lover."
3. **Day 3:** Look at five shops selling something similar. Note how they photograph and describe products. Don't copy designs.
4. **Day 4:** Choose three to five products for the first version.
5. **Day 5:** Choose your platform: Etsy if you want a marketplace, Shopify if you want your own store.
6. **Day 6:** Write your finish line and put it where you'll see it.
7. **Day 7:** Rest.

## Week 2: make the listings, one piece at a time

A listing has a few parts: photos, a title, a description, a price and a shipping plan. Take one part per day instead of finishing a whole listing in a night.

| Day | Move | Lighter 15-minute option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Photograph product 1 | Take three photos on your phone near a window |
| 9 | Write the title and description for product 1 | Write only the title |
| 10 | Photograph product 2 | Clear a clean background |
| 11 | Write product 2's listing | Write one honest sentence about it |
| 12 | Photograph and write product 3 | Photograph only |
| 13 | Decide your prices and check them against your costs | Write down what each item costs you to make |
| 14 | Rest | Rest |

Write descriptions in plain words: what it is, the size, what it's made of, how long it takes to ship. A buyer who can picture it will trust it more than one reading clever copy.

If you only have 15 minutes tonight, photograph one product near a window. One decent photo moves the store forward more than an hour of reading about photography.
{: .tip}

## Week 3: set up the shop itself

This is the unglamorous week, and it's where plans stall. Take one piece a day.

- **Day 15:** Create the account and shop name. Check the platform's current fees and setup costs on its own pages before you commit.
- **Day 16:** Add your first listing to the platform.
- **Day 17:** Add the other listings.
- **Day 18:** Set up how you get paid, including any identity checks the platform asks for.
- **Day 19:** Decide on shipping: who carries it, what you charge, how long it takes.
- **Day 20:** Place a test order, or have a friend do it, and check each step looks right.
- **Day 21:** Rest.

On Shopify, the [official setup guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-getting-started) starts with adding a first product, choosing a theme and setting up a domain, then points you to further setup steps. That order is a good model for any platform: product first, polish later.

## Week 4: open the store and tell people

Opening the store isn't the same as launching. Launching means people see it.

- **Day 22:** Read every page as a stranger would. Fix typos and broken details.
- **Day 23:** Open the store.
- **Day 24 to 26:** Message ten people, one or two a day. Send the link and ask them to look, and to tell you if anything is confusing.
- **Day 27:** Share it once where your buyers already are: a group, a social account, a newsletter.
- **Day 28:** Fix whatever your first visitors flagged.
- **Day 29:** Write down what happened: views, questions, anything you'd change.
- **Day 30:** Choose the next finish line, such as five more listings or your first sale.

Be gentle about the first sale. Printify's guide to the [first 30 days of an Etsy print-on-demand shop](https://printify.com/blog/first-30-days-as-an-etsy-pod-seller/) tells sellers to expect low traffic and possibly no sales early on, and to treat that as information. That guide is written by a print-on-demand vendor, so take its specifics with that in mind, but the advice to learn from the first weeks rather than judge yourself on them applies to any store.

## What to do when a day goes wrong

- **Slide, don't cram.** Missed a day? Move the plan down one.
- **Shrink before you quit.** If a week was rough, make next week's list shorter.
- **Keep the scope small.** New product ideas go on a "later" list. Our post on [one small step a day](/blog/one-small-step-a-day-finish-what-you-start/) shows how to keep a plan this small going.
- **Rest counts.** If you're running on empty, rest first and plan second.

1. Write your finish line.
2. Choose three products.
3. Mark four rest days on a calendar.
4. Decide tomorrow's Move: 15 or 30 minutes.
