Habitica is a free, game-style app that turns your habits, dailies and to-dos into a retro RPG with rewards. FollowThru is a project coach that gives you one small Move a day. Pick Habitica if game rewards motivate you. Pick FollowThru if you are building one project and want a clear next step.

If you are building something after work, you have probably tried a to-do app at some point. Lists are good at holding tasks. They are not always good at telling you which task matters tonight, or at noticing when you have gone quiet for a week.

So the useful question is not "which app is better?" It is "what is actually stopping me?" This post compares the two fairly. (We make FollowThru: it is the accountability app for people building something of their own. Habitica is made by someone else, and we only describe what we saw on its own listing.)

- Habitica gamifies tasks with an avatar, gold, gear and pets
- FollowThru picks one small project step a day and checks in each evening
- Habitica suits people who love game rewards; FollowThru suits builders who need a next step
- You can try one for two weeks and add the other only if something is missing

## What is Habitica good at?

According to its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habitica-gamified-taskmanager/id994882113), Habitica is a "free habit-building and productivity app that uses retro RPG elements." You create an avatar, add tasks, and earn gold, experience and in-game items when you complete real-world activities.

The listing describes repeating daily, weekly and monthly tasks, a flexible habit tracker, a classic to-do list, color-coded tasks, collectible gear and pets, and reminders and widgets. It also mentions Parties, Challenges and Guilds if you want social play.

It can be used fully for free, with optional in-app purchases. The listing says Habitica is open source and run by a small team, and that tasks are private.

That is a lot of well-made structure. If you enjoy collecting things and watching a level bar fill, Habitica makes boring tasks feel like a game. It also covers the whole of life: laundry, workouts, emails and your side project all live in one list.

## What is FollowThru good at?

FollowThru starts from a different problem: you know what you want to build, but not what to do tonight.

Each day you pick one small **Move** (15, 30 or 60 minutes). Tap **Start now** and **Momo**, a friendly sloth coach, breaks it into steps with a timer, drafts writing with you, asks sharp questions when you are deciding, and can write the prompt for your AI tool.

Momo checks in each evening with a short chat and remembers your project. Every Sunday you get a Weekly Builder Report: what you shipped, a pattern Momo noticed, and next week's focus with three Moves ready. Progress is **momentum, not streaks**, so a missed day does not reset anything.

It is in free beta, so it is newer and less proven than a long-running app. It is also built for one project at a time, not for tracking your whole life.

## Where do they differ most?

**Game rewards vs a clear next step.** Habitica rewards you for finishing tasks you already wrote down. FollowThru helps you decide which small step to take and then helps you do it. If your list is clear and you just need a nudge, a game may be enough. If your list says "launch shop" and nothing else, a smaller step helps more.

**Streaks vs momentum.** Habitica's listing mentions streak counters. If a broken chain would make you want to quit, you may prefer momentum, which forgives a missed day. Our post on [habit trackers vs. accountability coaches](/blog/habit-tracker-vs-accountability-coach/) goes deeper.

**Everything vs one thing.** Habitica holds all your tasks. FollowThru holds one project and remembers it, so the evening chat can ask about yesterday's Move rather than a generic "how was your day?"

## Which one fits your situation?

Here are four examples:

- **A mug shop owner** with a full list of tasks and no sense of what to do first: FollowThru gives one Move a day, such as "photograph three mugs against a plain wall."
- **A YouTube cooking channel** with a long to-do list and a love of rewards: Habitica can make the recurring chores (filming days, editing slots) feel like a game, and FollowThru can pick the next piece of the next video.
- **An app maker** who is stuck at 80%: FollowThru's Moves and Momo's prompt help can turn "finish the app" into a small testable step.
- **Someone who just likes quests and loot:** Habitica. That is a perfectly good reason.

## Can you use both?

Yes. They do different jobs. A game-style list can hold daily routines and a project coach can hold the one thing you are making. If it feels like too much, try one for two weeks, notice what is still missing, and then decide.

Here is a simple way to run the test:

1. Pick one app and use it for 14 days.
2. At the end of each week, ask: "Did I do something on my project this week?"
3. If yes, stay. If no, ask what was missing: a reward, a next step or a reminder.
4. Add or switch only if you can name the gap.

Rest is part of the plan too. A quiet week does not mean the experiment failed.

When you pick a tool, start with the smallest version of your project. The [one small step a day](/blog/one-small-step-a-day-finish-what-you-start/) approach works in any app, or in a notebook.

Choose your Move before you open any app. Write it as a sentence with a verb and a finish, such as "write the first three lines of the product description," and you will have a next step in any tool.
