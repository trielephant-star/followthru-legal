Focusmate matches you with a partner for a live video session of 25, 50 or 75 minutes, so you work side by side. FollowThru is a project coach that gives you one small Move a day. Pick Focusmate if starting is your hurdle. Pick FollowThru if knowing what to do is.

If you build after work, you know the two different ways an evening can go wrong. Sometimes you know exactly what to do and cannot make yourself sit down. Other times you sit down and stare, because "grow the shop" is not a task.

Those are different problems, and these two tools aim at different ones. This post compares them fairly. (We make FollowThru: it is the accountability app for people building something of their own. Focusmate is made by someone else, and we only describe what we saw on its own pages.)

- Focusmate gives you a live partner on video while you work
- FollowThru picks one small project step a day and checks in each evening
- Focusmate helps you start; FollowThru helps you decide what to start
- They work well together: choose the Move, then book the session

## What is Focusmate good at?

Focusmate describes itself as ["virtual coworking for getting anything done"](https://www.focusmate.com). You book a session, get matched with another member, greet each other and share your goals, then work for 25, 50 or 75 minutes. At the end you check in on how it went.

According to its [FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq), you work on your own task, and you can use it for most kinds of work as long as you stay on camera. A camera is required, and you keep your video on throughout. Your partner is working on their own thing, not yours.

The FAQ says Focusmate is completely free for up to three sessions per week, and a paid plan offers unlimited sessions. It runs in your browser, and the FAQ says there is no app, though you can add it to your phone's home screen.

The idea behind it is simple and kind: working near someone else who is focused can make sitting down easier. If the hardest part of your evening is the first five minutes, a person on a screen who expects you at 8:00 is a strong nudge.

## What is FollowThru good at?

FollowThru starts from the other problem: you know what you want to build, but not what to do tonight.

Each day you pick one small **Move** (15, 30 or 60 minutes). Tap **Start now** and **Momo**, a friendly sloth coach, breaks it into steps with a timer, drafts writing with you, asks sharp questions when you are deciding, and can write the prompt for your AI tool.

Momo checks in each evening with a short chat and remembers your project. Every Sunday you get a Weekly Builder Report: what you shipped, a pattern Momo noticed, and next week's focus with three Moves ready. Progress is **momentum, not streaks**, so a missed day does not reset anything.

It is in free beta, so it is newer and less proven than a long-running service. It is also built for one project at a time, and it does not put another person in the room with you.

## Where do they differ most?

**A person beside you vs a coach that knows your project.** Focusmate's partner is a stranger working on their own task. That is the point: their presence helps. They will not know that your shop's product photos are the thing blocking you. Momo does, because he remembers the project from one day to the next.

**Starting vs choosing.** Focusmate asks you to arrive with a goal. If you already have one, great. If your list says "launch shop" and nothing smaller, the session may start with ten minutes of deciding. A Move gives you the goal before you arrive.

**Camera on vs camera off.** Some people love being seen working. Others find a live video call at 9 p.m. tiring, or cannot be on camera at home. FollowThru runs without a partner or a camera.

**Booked time vs flexible time.** A Focusmate session is a time you book. FollowThru is a Move you can do in 15 minutes when a gap appears. If your evenings are unpredictable, that may matter.

## Which one fits your situation?

- **A mug shop owner** who never gets to the photos: a Focusmate session is a good way to finally shoot them. FollowThru can pick "photograph three mugs against a plain wall" as the Move.
- **A YouTube cooking channel** that struggles to start editing: book a 50-minute session and edit while someone else works beside you.
- **An app maker** stuck at 80% with no clear next step: FollowThru's Moves and Momo's prompt help turn "finish the app" into something testable.
- **Someone who works alone all day and misses company:** Focusmate. That is a perfectly good reason.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and they fit together neatly. Here is one way to run it:

1. In the afternoon, pick tonight's Move (one small sentence with a verb and a finish).
2. Book a Focusmate session for the time you will do it.
3. Tell your partner the Move as your goal at the start.
4. When the session ends, log the Move and let the evening check-in close the day.
5. On Sunday, look at the week and pick three Moves for next week.

If that is too much, use one for two weeks and ask: "Did I do something on my project this week? If not, what was missing: company, a next step or a reminder?" Add the second tool only when you can name the gap. Rest is part of the plan too, and a quiet week does not mean the experiment failed.

For more on picking a small enough step, see [one small step a day](/blog/one-small-step-a-day-finish-what-you-start/). For a wider look at tools, see [the best accountability apps for side projects](/blog/best-accountability-apps-for-side-projects/).

If you book a live session, write your goal as something you can finish in that time, such as "write the first three lines of the product description", not "work on the shop". Your partner can then cheer a finish, and you will know it happened.
